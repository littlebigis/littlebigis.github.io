Saṃsāra: The Cycle of Rebirth

Saṃsāra means "wandering" or "world", with the connotation of cyclic, circuitous change. It refers to the theory of rebirth and "cyclicality of all life, matter, existence". Saṃsāra is considered to be dukkha, unsatisfactory, and painful. Because life, and continuously being reborn according to your karma results in suffering, the main goal of Buddhism is to attain nirvana, or to break the constant cycle of rebirth.

In Buddhism, Saṃsāra has no beginning or end. You are born, you live, you die, and then you are reborn again into one of the 6 realms of existence. There are 3 good realms and 3 bad realms. The three good realms are the heavenly, demi-god, and human realms. The three bad or evil realms are the animal, ghost, and hell realms. Over your various lifetimes, you accrue karma based on how you lived your life. If you live according to The Noble Eightfold Path, you will accrue good karma. If you live a life where you give into desire and hurt others, you will accumulate bad karma. You will be reborn into a realm appropriate to your karma. It is widely considered a great privilege to be born into the human realm. As humans, we have the teachings of the Buddha as well as the intelligence to understand the teachings. We have the greatest chance of becoming enlightened and attaining nirvana. On the other hand, the animal realm is considered bad because animals do not necessarily have the intelligence to understand and live by the Dharma, therefore animals have a low chance of attaining nirvana or even moving to a higher realm.
