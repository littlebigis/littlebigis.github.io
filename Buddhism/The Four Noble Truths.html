The Four Noble Truths

The Four Noble Truths are the foundation of Buddhism, taught by Gautama Buddha in his first sermon. They explain the nature of human existance and how to attain enlightenment.


1. Dukkha: Suffering

Dukkha translates to suffering.
The first noble truth is that suffering (pervasive dissatisfaction, unease, and pain) exists in life. Suffering can take the form of physical and emotional pain (including birth,aging, sickness, and death), as well as the deeper anxiety of change, impermanence, and unfulfilled expectations.


2. Samudāya: The Origin of Suffering

The second noble truth is that there is an origin to suffering. It is rooted in craving, grasping, and thirst (tanhā), which are fueled by fundamental ignorance and the three poisons: greed, hatred, and delusion.


3. Nirodha: The Cessation of Suffering

By letting go of craving and attachment, one can reach Nirvana—a state of complete peace, freedom from negative emotions, and release from the cycle of rebirth (samsara).


4. Magga: The Path to the Cessation of Suffering (The Noble Eightfold Path)

The Buddha provided a path that we can follow in order to liberate ourselves from suffering. This path has 8 different steps broken into 3 categories:
Wisdom (Prajñā): Right view and right intention.
Ethical Conduct (Sīla): Right speech, right action, and right livelihood.
Mental Discipline (Samādhi): Right effort, right mindfulness, and right concentration.
